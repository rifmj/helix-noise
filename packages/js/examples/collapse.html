Self-similar collapse — collapse, dssCollapse

A time warp: not new content, a new way of moving through what is already there. collapse shrinks the whole pattern toward a point like (T−t)^q while speeding it up like (T−t)^(q−1) — focus and acceleration locked together, so it reads as gathering rather than as a zoom. Scaling velocity by A, vorticity by A/L and the potential by A·L keeps the field exactly divergence-free at every instant; that guarantee cannot be broken by any setting on this page.

dssCollapse adds the interesting part. Driven by the renormalisation time s = −log(T−t) with 1-periodic modulation, advancing s by log λ returns the field to exactly what it was, rescaled by λ^(−b) in space and λ^a in amplitude. So one period tiles the entire collapse — an infinite zoom with no cross-fade and no drift. The three panels check it live: now, one period later rescaled, and their difference, with the measured error printed. It reads ~1e-16. Turn off frozen profile and watch the loop deliberately fail.
