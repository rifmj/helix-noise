Portal — createRing, strainedColumn, compose, collapse

A game effect assembled out of the library's closed-form pieces instead of a solver. createRing is the rim: a torus of swirl that is exactly zero outside its own tube, so it decorates the edge and touches nothing else. strainedColumn is the throat — an exact stationary Navier–Stokes solution, so the intake is not art-directed: the inward pull u^r = −a·r, the spin u^θ = ε(1−e^(−a r²/2ν))/r and the axial draw u^z = 2a(z−z₀) all come from one balance, and raising the strain makes the throat narrower and faster at the same time because that is what the solution does. compose stacks them with a broadband layer for detail. Drag to orbit.

The charge is a state machine driven by parameter ramps, not a cross-dissolve. Going from idle to open, the strain a climbs ×8.3, the spin ε ×6.7, the rim circulation Γ ×5.6, and the detail layer is wrapped in collapse whose remaining time τ = T−t falls from 1 to 0.1 — so the texture shrinks toward the portal like τ^0.6 while its amplitude grows like τ^(−0.4). Nothing fades in; the motion changes because the field changes. log-periodic swaps collapse for dssCollapse, which makes the gather pulse once per λ-fold of the zoom instead of climbing smoothly.

The number on the page. Everything the portal does rests on one guarantee — the composed field is divergence-free — so that is what the readout measures. It prints the trace of the analytic velocity gradient, ∇·u = tr ∇u, sampled at 96 fixed points through the rendered volume with field.sampleGrad: the same nine numbers that produce the shear, not a finite difference with a step size to argue about. Against the field's own gradient magnitude it reads ~1e-16 at every charge state and every slider position.

A zero that cannot move is not evidence, so there is a switch that moves it. + artist intake adds the layer a VFX artist reaches for first: a radial pull toward the portal centre, u = −k·e^(−ρ²/2w²)·(x−c). It is a perfectly reasonable-looking suck, it has no curl representation, and its divergence is −k·e^(−ρ²/2w²)(3 − ρ²/w²) — order one. Switch it on and the receipt goes from 1e-16 to ~1e-1 relative, ×10¹⁵, while the picture quietly stops working: the funnel loses its through-flow and the particles pile into a ball at the centre instead of passing through. That failure is what the number is for.

The throat's scales are printed, not eyeballed. columnCore(a, ν) = √(2ν/a) is the radius where the column's Gaussian vorticity has fallen to 1/e, and columnPeakVorticity(a, ν, ε) = εa/ν is its value on the axis. The wireframe cylinder is drawn at exactly that radius when scale guides is on, so as you raise the strain you can watch the bright core shrink onto a guide whose number is on screen. Beside it the readout prints the largest |ω| any particle is actually sitting in, and that one is normally larger — the rim tube's own vorticity runs to ≈ 12Γ/c² + Γ/R², several times the throat's peak. Two different structures, two different scales, both from closed forms; the readout does not pretend the throat owns the whole frame, only the axis.

What this is not. It is a kinematic velocity field: there is no pressure solve, no collision, no boundary condition and no free surface, and the only viscosity anywhere is the ν that strainedColumn needs to define its own core. Particles are massless tracers — they take the field's velocity exactly, with no inertia and no drag. Each frame's field is a valid stationary solution, but sliding along a family of stationary solutions is not a solution of the unsteady equations: a real vortex spun up this fast would not stay Burgers-shaped. The glowing disc and the rim tube are sprites and do not move anything; every particle you see is moving because sampleUW said so.
