Streamline, pathline, streakline — churn, decay, exactNS

The most-taught figure in flow visualisation, and the one thing 27 demos never drew: the three curve families through a single point. A dye nozzle sits in the flow. The streamline is the curve tangent to the velocity field right now; the pathline is where one particle released at the nozzle actually went; the streakline is the plume — every particle ever released, at the positions they occupy now. In a steady flow all three coincide. The textbook shorthand is that unsteadiness pulls them apart.

That shorthand is wrong, and this library can refute it on screen. Row 2 is exactNS — a genuinely unsteady, genuinely viscous, exact Navier–Stokes solution. Its velocity changes by a third over the window measured in column A. Yet the three curves stay on top of each other to twelve decimal places, because a single-shell Beltrami field decays as one global scalar e^(−νk₀²t): every mode shares one |k|, so the field's direction never moves even as its magnitude collapses. Reparametrise time and the pathlines are the streamlines. Row 3 gives the same viscosity a broadband spectrum instead — the modes now decay at different rates, the direction field turns, and the curves separate. So the separation is about the spectrum, not about unsteadiness, and row 3 is what stops row 2 from being a claim about decay in general.
